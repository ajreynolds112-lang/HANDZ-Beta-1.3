Tier 1 — Governing Fight Philosophy
#	Fundamental	Meaning for the AI
1	Continuous Advantage (continuous_advantage)	Every action should gain position, information, initiative, stamina advantage, damage, or momentum. There is no truly neutral action. This combines your “gaining ground,” “small victories,” and momentum ideas.
2	Defensive Responsibility (defensive_responsibility)	Offense should never abandon defense, distance management, balance, or awareness of the return shot.
3	Decision Quality Under Pressure (pressure_decision_quality)	Winning ultimately means choosing the highest-value action while threatened, tired, hurt, or rushed.
4	Combat Composure (combat_composure)	Calmness improves perception, timing, patience, and opportunity recognition.
5	Constraint Control (constraint_control)	Control increases as the opponent loses viable options: movement, rhythm, timing, preferred punches, safe exits, etc.
6	Active Patience (active_patience)	Patience does not mean inactivity. Create reactions, gather information, threaten, and wait for favorable commitments.
7	Style Plasticity (style_plasticity)	The AI should be able to change style rather than remain permanently aggressive, defensive, counter-oriented, or evasive.
8	Layered Adaptation (layered_adaptation)	Continuously make small adjustments, and when the opponent adapts, answer with multiple interacting adaptations rather than one predictable response.
Tier 2 — Fight Intelligence & Prediction
#	Fundamental	Meaning for the AI
9	Information Harvesting (information_harvesting)	Early actions should reveal range, reactions, habits, defensive preferences, counters, and tendencies.
10	Predictive Timing (predictive_timing)	Timing is pattern recognition plus prediction: determine when the opponent is likely to commit, move, punch, defend, or reset. This absorbs “timing beats speed.”
11	Habit Exploitation (habit_exploitation)	Detect repeated behaviors, bad habits, predictable counters, and overcommitments and construct punishments for them.
12	Threat Assumption (threat_assumption)	Behave as though the opponent is always attempting to set up a counter or trap until evidence says otherwise.
13	Impact Diagnosis (impact_diagnosis)	After getting hit, identify the cause: range error, timing error, careless offense, wrong defense, bad defensive timing, etc., then alter behavior.
14	Defense Vulnerability Modeling (defense_vulnerability_model)	Every defensive system exposes something. Identify what the opponent's chosen defense gives up.
15	Counter-Threat Mapping (counter_threat_mapping)	Predict which counter is most dangerous given your intended punch, position, distance, and opponent tendencies, then select defense accordingly.
16	Risk-Reward Counter Selection (counter_expected_value)	Prefer counters that maximize damage and positional gain relative to the danger accepted.
Tier 3 — Strategic Control of the Fight
#	Fundamental	Meaning for the AI
17	Initiative Priority (initiative_priority)	Being prepared to attack or defend before the opponent gives control over what happens next.
18	Ring Generalship (ring_generalship)	Control when and where exchanges occur rather than merely winning individual punch interactions.
19	Tempo Control (tempo_control)	Control the speed and intensity of the fight moment-to-moment. Do not merely synchronize yourself to the opponent.
20	Momentum Management (momentum_management)	Recognize and compound favorable sequences while interrupting the opponent's momentum.
21	Pressure With Responsibility (responsible_pressure)	Pressure should disrupt decisions, stamina, rhythm, and timing without advancing faster than the AI can safely defend.
22	Reset Advantage (reset_advantage)	Position is partly measured by who needs less time to become fully offensive/defensive again. Reset before the opponent and exploit their reset.
23	Reset Exploitation (reset_exploitation)	Recognize the instant an opponent mentally or physically believes an exchange is finished and attack that transition.
24	Advantage Window Exploitation (advantage_window)	Enter, capitalize, and leave before the opponent can recover sufficiently to retaliate.
Tier 4 — Position, Distance & Exchange Structure
#	Fundamental	Meaning for the AI
25	Range Elasticity (range_elasticity)	Distance control is not simply standing at the correct range; it is the ability to enter and leave that range faster than the opponent can respond.
26	Step Timing (step_timing)	Time the opponent's foot movement to manipulate distance rather than judging range from static coordinates alone.
27	Angle Dominance (angle_dominance)	Seek positions where your own attack is reliable while the opponent's return attack is mechanically disadvantaged.
28	Lead-Hand Dominance (lead_hand_dominance)	Treat control of the opponent's lead hand as a positional and information advantage.
29	Ring Interception (ring_interception)	Cut the ring by moving laterally to intercept the opponent's projected escape path instead of merely following them.
30	Balance Integrity (balance_integrity)	Preserve a state from which offense, defense, movement, or exit can immediately continue. Landing while balanced is evidence of responsible range and position.
31	Post-Action Positioning (post_action_positioning)	Evaluate punches, steps, and defensive actions partly by where they leave the fighter afterward.
32	Functional Punch Effect (functional_punch_effect)	A punch can succeed without landing cleanly if it forces a reset, moves the opponent, changes their guard, or creates the next attack.
33	Positional Shot Selection (positional_shot_selection)	Accuracy means selecting punches appropriate to travel distance, openings, and current positioning—not simply aiming correctly.
34	Exchange Cycle (exchange_cycle)	Model combat as Entry → Attack → Transition → Exit, rather than treating punches as independent actions.
35	Combat State Transition (combat_state_transition)	Seamlessly switch offense → defense → recovery → offense while preserving balance and positioning.
36	Exit-to-Reentry Chaining (exit_to_reentry)	An exit should simultaneously position the fighter for the next exchange, ideally where they can reset first.
Tier 5 — Defensive & Counterpunching Architecture
#	Fundamental	Meaning for the AI
37	Active Defense (active_defense)	Defense should change position, distance, angle, rhythm, or initiative rather than remaining stationary and absorbing attacks.
38	Distance-First Defense (distance_first_defense)	Distance is the first defensive layer; blocking and slipping become more important when position or distance has already failed.
39	Integrated Evasion (integrated_evasion)	Defense should occur before, during, and after offense; head movement and punching should be integrated rather than sequentially isolated.
40	Return-Fire Expectation (return_fire_expectation)	As an attack reaches completion, immediately anticipate the opponent's counter window.
41	Dead-Zone Avoidance (dead_zone_avoidance)	The location from which you just finished attacking is dangerous; move, defend, counter, or deliberately use it as bait.
42	Counter Window Recognition (counter_window_recognition)	An opponent's punch temporarily opens a counter window that shrinks as their hand and guard recover.
43	Counter Window Creation (counter_window_creation)	Do not merely wait for counter opportunities; manufacture them through feints and bait.
44	Defensive Variation (defensive_variation)	After repeated defensive actions, alter movement, range, counter, or defensive method so the defense itself does not become predictable.
45	Counter Variation (counter_variation)	Rotate counter types because predictable counters themselves can be trapped.
46	Commitment Evasion (commitment_evasion)	Movement becomes especially valuable once the opponent has committed to a punch or forward movement.
47	Make-Miss-Pay-Exit (make_miss_pay_exit)	Head movement is incomplete unless the miss is converted into a counter and followed by responsible repositioning.
48	Front-Time Limit (front_time_limit)	Taking multiple unanswered punches is evidence that the AI has remained directly available for too long; counter, angle, or disengage.
49	Punch Neutralization (punch_neutralization)	Neutralize a specific attack through the interaction of timing, distance, and angle rather than relying on one defensive move.
Tier 6 — Deception, Setups & Combination Design
#	Fundamental	Meaning for the AI
50	Reaction Engineering (reaction_engineering)	Feints and setup punches exist to cause a predictable reaction that the AI can exploit.
51	Feint Conversion (feint_conversion)	A feint should lead to a real punch or positional change; otherwise it becomes an empty threat.
52	Feint Credibility (feint_credibility)	A successful real attack makes its future feint believable; repeated success can therefore become the foundation of deception.
53	Misdirection (misdirection)	Establish the expectation of one action and deliberately produce another.
54	Trap Engineering (trap_engineering)	Exploit existing habits or deliberately display a fake vulnerability/bad habit while remaining prepared to punish the expected response.
55	Throwaway Setup (throwaway_setup)	Use a low-commitment punch primarily to capture attention or elicit a defensive reaction for the real attack.
56	Three-Layer Combination (setup_distraction_finish)	Structure combinations as setup → distraction/reaction → intended scoring punch.
57	Angular Combination Chaining (angular_combination_chaining)	Combination design should include positional and angular changes instead of being only a sequence of punches.
58	Layered Entry (layered_entry)	Work into range using combinations of feints, setups, explosive attacks, lead-hand control, counters, and angles.
59	Rhythm Variability (rhythm_variability)	Prevent the opponent from knowing when punches, steps, combinations, or exits begin and end.
Tier 7 — Specific Tactical Fundamentals
#	Fundamental	Meaning for the AI
60	Opening Calibration (opening_calibration)	Use early punches partly to establish range and observe defensive reactions.
61	Finish Discipline (finish_discipline)	When the opponent is hurt, increase exploitation without abandoning defense or blindly rushing.
62	Recovery Jab (recovery_jab)	When tired, use the jab as a distance-management action that can create recovery time.
63	Miss Recovery (miss_recovery)	Treat a missed punch as a positional error requiring immediate recovery, defense, or movement.
64	Back-Foot Invitation (back_foot_invitation)	Yield apparent initiative to encourage the opponent forward, then exploit their forward commitment with footwork and counters.
65	Double-Jab Disruption (double_jab_disruption)	Use the first jab to provoke/occupy the defense and the second to exploit the resulting reaction while disturbing rhythm.
66	Check-Hook Intercept (check_hook_intercept)	Punish careless forward rushing before the opponent reaches smothering range.
67	Close-Miss Countering (close_miss_countering)	Evade by the minimum distance necessary so the fighter remains close enough to counter before the attacking hand recovers.
68	Lead-Hand Occupation (lead_hand_occupation)	Manipulate or occupy the opponent's lead hand to temporarily remove it as an offensive/defensive option.
69	Hand-Check Rhythm Disruption (hand_check_disruption)	Use feinted glove contact from appropriate range to interrupt timing while remaining defensively responsible.
70	Pull Counter (pull_counter)	Bait the straight, evade backward at commitment, fire the cross during recovery, then roll/exit.
71	Attrition Targeting (attrition_targeting)	Deliberately target areas such as the body or shoulders when the goal is degrading stamina or defensive effectiveness rather than immediate head damage.
72	Emergency Blocking (emergency_blocking)	Blocking becomes the fallback defensive solution when distance positioning has already failed.
73	Forced Readjustment (forced_readjustment)	Favor attacks that force the opponent to reposition, re-guard, or otherwise reset before acting again.
